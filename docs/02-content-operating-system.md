# 02 — Content Operating System (Content OS)

## Purpose

Create a repeatable, auditable process that converts customer inputs into useful content without treating raw AI output as a final deliverable.

## Content lifecycle

**Intake → Diagnose → Strategy → Draft → Produce → Review → Revise → Deliver → Learn**

### 1. Intake

Required brief fields:
- Business / product / offer.
- Audience and market.
- Goal: awareness, education, consideration, conversion, retention, or other.
- Platform and placement.
- Desired format and length.
- Brand voice and words to use/avoid.
- Key facts and proof the customer can substantiate.
- CTA and destination.
- References and rights confirmation.
- Restrictions, deadline, and reviewer.
- Success measure (if available).

If a required fact is unknown, label it unknown and ask the customer or omit it. Do not fill gaps with invented claims.

### 2. Diagnose

Create a brief summary and list:
- What is known.
- What is missing.
- Potential risks or prohibited claims.
- The intended audience insight.
- The single primary message.
- Required approval questions.

Pause production when missing information could materially change the output.

### 3. Strategy

For each deliverable define:
- Audience stage.
- Content angle.
- Hook / opening.
- Core message.
- Evidence or demonstration.
- CTA.
- Platform constraints.
- Why this angle fits the brief.

Generate alternatives only when they are meaningfully different, not superficial rewrites.

### 4. Draft and production

Use the task-specific prompt template and customer-approved references. Generate text or creative assets in stages. Preserve the approved brief and prompt version for traceability. Tool outputs are drafts.

### 5. Human quality control

Score each dimension from 0 to 2:
- Brief alignment.
- Factual support.
- Audience relevance.
- Clarity and structure.
- Originality / non-generic value.
- Brand voice.
- Platform fit.
- CTA clarity.
- Rights / safety / policy.
- File and formatting correctness.

Maximum: 20. Proposed release threshold: 17/20, with no zero in factual support, rights/safety, or brief alignment. This threshold is an initial operating rule and should be adjusted after real pilots.

### 6. Revision

Classify feedback as:
- Brief mismatch.
- Factual correction.
- Tone / style adjustment.
- Format / technical issue.
- New scope request.

Correct errors without charging a revision where the delivered work failed agreed criteria. Treat a new direction or extra deliverable as a scope change, disclosed before proceeding.

### 7. Delivery

Deliver:
- Clearly named files.
- A manifest listing each asset and format.
- Any assumptions or placeholders.
- Usage notes where useful.
- A short revision instruction and deadline.
- Confirmation of what is and is not included.

### 8. Learning loop

Record actual time, direct costs, rework reason, QC score, delivery timeliness, feedback, and repeat-purchase signals. Use aggregate learning to improve prompts and offers; do not expose one customer's confidential material to another.

## Initial content formats

Begin with text-first formats that are cheap to produce and easy to QA:
- Short-video scripts.
- Hooks and opening lines.
- Captions and CTA variants.
- Content calendars / idea batches.
- Product storytelling angles.
- Repurposed content from customer-owned source material.

Treat generated images and video as optional production modules. Verify model access, generation limits, output rights, watermarking, and actual cost before promising them.

## Content rules

- No invented statistics, testimonials, product features, certifications, guarantees, or customer results.
- Cite or link sources when factual claims require support.
- Do not imitate a living creator's exact style; translate references into high-level attributes.
- Do not reuse competitor copy or customer assets without a valid basis.
- Label speculative angles as concepts.
- Respect platform policies and applicable law.
- Use customer-specific data only for the agreed project.

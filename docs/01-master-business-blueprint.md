# 01 — Master Business Blueprint

## Executive summary

Embrilo is an early-stage content production service. It uses AI tools to accelerate research, ideation, drafting, and creative production while retaining human control over strategy, factual accuracy, brand fit, and final delivery.

The immediate objective is not to launch a complex platform. It is to prove that a specific customer segment will pay for a defined content outcome, deliver that outcome consistently, and understand the true cost and time required.

## Problem hypothesis

Small businesses and individual sellers may need regular short-form content but lack some combination of time, creative confidence, scripting skill, editing capacity, or a repeatable process. This is a hypothesis to validate, not an established market fact.

## Initial customer hypotheses

Evaluate rather than assume:
1. Small online sellers that need product storytelling.
2. Local service businesses that need social content.
3. Solo creators or personal brands that need script and content repurposing support.

Choose one segment after comparing access to buyers, urgency, willingness to pay, repeatability, content complexity, and ability to demonstrate results honestly.

## Proposed value proposition

“Embrilo helps turn your brief into ready-to-review content faster, combining AI-assisted production with human quality control.”

Avoid promises such as guaranteed virality, guaranteed sales, fully automated perfection, or specific turnaround until measured.

## Initial service menu (hypotheses)

Start with one offer, not a broad agency catalogue:
- **Content Starter:** a small batch of content ideas, hooks, captions, and short-video scripts based on one brief.
- **Product Story Pack:** product positioning angles, scripts, captions, and CTA variants for one product.
- **Repurpose Pack:** adapt customer-provided long-form material into shorter platform-specific drafts, subject to rights and source quality.

Do not sell video editing, finished AI video, voiceover, publishing, or ad management until tools, rights, turnaround, and costs have been tested.

## Offer design checklist

Each offer must state:
- Customer and problem it serves.
- Exact inputs required.
- Exact outputs and file formats.
- Quantity and length limits.
- Number of revision rounds.
- Delivery window and what can delay it.
- Customer responsibilities and content rights.
- Exclusions and refund/cancellation terms.
- Price, payment timing, and any third-party cost.
- Acceptance criteria.

## Business model

Initial model: fixed-scope service packages. A quote-based custom service can be offered later, but every project must have a written scope. Recurring packages are a later option only if repeat demand is demonstrated.

## Delivery flow

1. Customer sees a clear offer and sample labelled as a concept.
2. Customer submits brief and references they have rights to use.
3. Embrilo checks completeness and feasibility.
4. Embrilo confirms scope, price, timeline, and revisions.
5. Customer approves terms and pays through a configured method.
6. Production begins; AI drafts are generated using approved templates.
7. Human quality review and correction.
8. Deliver files plus a concise handover note.
9. Customer requests permitted revisions or accepts.
10. Record time, direct costs, quality issues, and feedback.

## Cost and pricing model

Do not set a final price by copying a competitor. For each package calculate:

- Direct tool cost per order.
- Payment processing cost.
- Production hours × target hourly contribution.
- Revision allowance.
- Storage/transfer costs, if any.
- Expected rework and support time.
- Tax and other applicable costs.

Minimum viable price should cover realistic delivery cost and a reasonable contribution. Validate willingness to pay with real conversations and paid pilots; do not fabricate conversion evidence.

## Evidence plan

Collect evidence from public offers, official pricing pages, transparent competitor samples, and real customer conversations. Keep a research log with URL, access date, exact observation, limitations, and decision affected. Public comments or search volume alone do not prove willingness to pay.

## Acquisition approach

Start with low-cost proof:
- Publish clearly labelled concept samples for one niche.
- Explain the before/after transformation of a brief into a deliverable.
- Use a small public portfolio with no invented client claims.
- Invite feedback from relevant communities without spam or unsolicited bulk messaging.
- Track qualified interest, brief submissions, quote acceptance, paid orders, and repeat interest.

## Main risks

- No validated buyer or offer.
- AI output sounds generic or inaccurate.
- Content uses assets without permission.
- Scope creep and unlimited revisions.
- Tool plans or model access change.
- Underpricing due to hidden labor.
- Customer briefs contain confidential data.
- Payment or delivery is promised before a system exists.

## First decision gate

Do not build a full SaaS until Embrilo has completed at least one end-to-end manual delivery and recorded:
- Brief completeness.
- Actual production and review time.
- Direct cost.
- Customer acceptance and revision count.
- Customer feedback and evidence of willingness to pay.

This is a proposed gate, not a claim that any order has already been delivered.

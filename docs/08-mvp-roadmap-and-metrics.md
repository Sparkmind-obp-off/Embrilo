# 08 — MVP Roadmap and North Star Metrics

## North Star Metric

**Qualified Deliveries Accepted (QDA):** count of paid projects delivered within the agreed scope that the customer accepts, or that pass the explicitly agreed acceptance process, during a reporting period.

Why this metric: it connects revenue to completed customer value. Do not count free samples, unpaid experiments, cancelled orders, or drafts that failed QC.

Track alongside:
- Qualified inquiries.
- Brief completion rate.
- Quote acceptance rate.
- Paid conversion rate.
- On-time delivery rate.
- Average production + QC hours per order.
- Direct cost per order.
- Revision rate and reasons.
- Customer satisfaction / qualitative feedback.
- Repeat purchase rate.
- Contribution after direct delivery costs.

No baseline or target is set because there is no verified operating data yet. Set targets after a small pilot cohort.

## Phase 0 — Evidence and feasibility

Deliver:
- Source of truth and business blueprint.
- One target-segment comparison.
- Competitor and public-offer research log.
- One clearly scoped pilot offer.
- Pricing worksheet with actual time/cost assumptions.
- Manual brief, prompt, QC, and delivery templates.

Exit gate: owner can explain the customer, problem, exact output, price logic, and how to deliver it.

## Phase 1 — Manual service validation

Deliver:
- A small concept portfolio labelled honestly.
- Intake form / brief template.
- Written quote and terms.
- Manual AI production bridge.
- Human QC and delivery checklist.
- Feedback capture.

Exit gate: complete at least one real end-to-end paid pilot or record why demand failed to materialize. Do not describe an unpaid sample as proof of willingness to pay.

## Phase 2 — Operational MVP

Deliver:
- Public website on the purchased domain.
- Reliable intake with spam controls.
- Internal project tracker.
- Clear status and delivery process.
- Basic backup/export and privacy procedures.

Exit gate: the owner can handle a project without losing the brief, scope, status, files, or review history.

## Phase 3 — Repeatability

Deliver:
- Refined offer based on feedback.
- Reusable prompt library and content templates.
- Measured production time and unit economics.
- Consistent portfolio and customer communication.
- A repeatable, non-spam acquisition channel.

Exit gate: evidence of repeat demand or a clear reason to adjust segment/offer.

## Phase 4 — Workflow automation

Consider:
- Genspark reusable Skills/Workflows where available.
- Notifications, intake routing, and metadata logging.
- Secure storage integrations.
- Payment provider integration with verified callbacks.

Exit gate: each automation saves measurable time, has an owner, and has failure/retry handling.

## Phase 5 — Productization

Only consider customer accounts, multi-tenant architecture, self-service ordering, subscriptions, and autonomous orchestration after repeatable demand, service economics, security, and support capacity justify them.

## Prioritization rubric

Score each proposed feature 1–5 for:
- Customer value.
- Frequency of use.
- Time saved.
- Confidence in evidence.
- Cost and implementation effort.
- Risk reduction.

Build the highest-value, highest-confidence items with low cost first. Do not prioritize a feature just because it is technically exciting.

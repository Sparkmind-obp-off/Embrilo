# Embrilo — Market Validation Research Plan

## Purpose

Find evidence for one target customer and one paid offer before investing in software or subscriptions.

## Research questions

1. Which customer segment has a frequent, specific content-production problem?
2. What do they currently do to solve it?
3. What do public competitors actually include in their offers?
4. What are publicly stated prices, limits, turnaround, and revision terms?
5. What evidence indicates active demand versus general interest?
6. What would make a customer trust Embrilo with a brief and brand?
7. What is the smallest useful paid outcome Embrilo can deliver?

## Candidate segments to compare

- Small online sellers.
- Local service businesses.
- Solo creators / personal brands.

These are hypotheses, not selected target segments.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Direct customer interviews and paid pilot behavior, with consent.
2. Official competitor service pages, price lists, terms, and portfolio samples.
3. Public marketplace listings and documented offers.
4. Public community posts describing a specific problem or purchase intent, respecting each platform's terms and privacy.
5. Search trends and general industry reports as supporting context only.

Do not scrape behind login walls, bypass platform restrictions, collect private group data without permission, or infer willingness to pay from likes alone.

## Competitor comparison fields

- Brand / provider.
- URL.
- Access date.
- Target customer.
- Deliverables.
- Public price and currency.
- Turnaround.
- Revision policy.
- Evidence of actual work (clearly distinguish concept work).
- Positioning and CTA.
- Limitations / unknowns.
- What Embrilo can learn without copying.

## Customer discovery questions

Ask open questions about recent behavior:
- Tell me about the last time you needed content for this product/service.
- What did you need to create and by when?
- How did you handle it?
- What was frustrating or costly?
- What happened when content was delayed or weak?
- Have you paid someone or used a tool for this? What did you pay and what did you receive?
- What would make a small content package useful enough to buy?
- What would make you hesitate?

Avoid leading questions such as “Would you buy my amazing AI service?”

## Validation experiment

1. Pick one segment and one narrow offer.
2. Produce a concept sample using synthetic or owner-owned material.
3. Label the sample as a concept, not a client result.
4. Present the exact scope and proposed price to relevant, consenting prospects.
5. Record qualified responses and objections.
6. If possible, run a small paid pilot with explicit scope and terms.
7. Record actual time, costs, revision count, satisfaction, and repeat interest.
8. Decide: proceed, adjust, or stop.

## Decision rule

Do not declare product-market fit from a handful of likes or compliments. A positive signal is a real paid pilot and a successful delivery; stronger evidence requires repeat demand across multiple customers and acceptable contribution margins.

## Research log

For each source, record: question, source URL, access date, exact observation, evidence class, limitation, and decision affected. Never claim that a domain or trademark is clear based only on a search returning no results.
